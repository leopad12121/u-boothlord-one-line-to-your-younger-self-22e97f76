# Candidates — One Line to Your Younger Self

Eight original candidate lines, each scored 1–5 on the judge's five criteria
(25 max). Criteria: (1) a single line actually told to a younger self;
(2) something only this person's older self could say, drawn from a life
actually lived; (3) lands in one breath, real voice, no explaining;
(4) a turn, image or small surprise that stays after one reading;
(5) honest and specific enough to sting a little, hard-won or warm
rather than sentimental.

## 1. — 22/25
> Kid, the girl who just laughed at your haircut will marry you in eleven years — stop hiding in the library.
- (1) 5 — directly addressed to "Kid", a younger self, not a slogan.
- (2) 4 — a named memory (haircut, library) only this older self could know.
- (3) 4 — one breath, plain voice, no explanation.
- (4) 5 — the laugh that becomes a marriage is a genuine turn.
- (5) 4 — warm and a little stinging: the hiding was the waste.

## 2. — 20/25
> Stop saving the good shirt for a special day; the special day is the Tuesday you finally wear it.
- (1) 4 — imperative to a younger self, but could address anyone.
- (2) 3 — a lived habit, but a fairly universal one.
- (3) 5 — lands in one breath, no explaining.
- (4) 4 — "the Tuesday" is a nice small surprise.
- (5) 4 — stings gently; the wasted waiting is real.

## 3. — 22/25
> The night you think ruined everything is the night you'll tell your kids about, laughing, twenty years from now.
- (1) 5 — "you" is clearly the younger self.
- (2) 4 — a specific night, known only in hindsight.
- (3) 4 — one breath, though "twenty years from now" slows it slightly.
- (4) 4 — ruin turning into a family story is a real turn.
- (5) 5 — hard-won and warm; the sting is the present pain.

## 4. — 17/25
> You are not behind — the boy who peaked at sixteen is still telling that story at forty.
- (1) 4 — addressed to the younger self.
- (2) 3 — the sixteen-year-old peaker is specific, but the advice is generic.
- (3) 4 — one breath, clean voice.
- (4) 3 — a mild turn, no image that lingers.
- (5) 3 — comforting rather than stinging.

## 5. — 22/25
> Call your grandmother back; one day the phone will ring and it won't be her.
- (1) 5 — a direct instruction to the younger self.
- (2) 4 — a lived loss, known only afterwards.
- (3) 5 — one breath, plain and urgent.
- (4) 3 — the image is strong but the turn is familiar.
- (5) 5 — honest, specific, and it stings.

## 6. — 18/25
> That job you didn't get? In five years you'll thank the door that stayed shut.
- (1) 4 — addressed to the younger self, but reads like advice for anyone.
- (2) 3 — a real rejection, but the consolation is a commonplace.
- (3) 4 — one breath, conversational.
- (4) 3 — the shut door is a familiar image.
- (5) 4 — warm, mildly stinging.

## 7. — 19/25
> Keep the letter. Burn the grudge. You'll need the first and forget the second.
- (1) 4 — imperative to the younger self.
- (2) 3 — a letter and a grudge, but no detail that pins them to one life.
- (3) 4 — short and punchy, though the two sentences split the breath.
- (4) 4 — the letter/grudge pairing is a neat turn.
- (5) 4 — hard-won, a little stinging.

## 8. — 20/25
> Stop rehearsing the apology — say it badly tonight, before the silence learns your name.
- (1) 5 — direct, urgent, to the younger self.
- (2) 4 — a specific unsaid apology, known only in hindsight.
- (3) 4 — one breath, real voice.
- (4) 4 — "before the silence learns your name" is a striking image.
- (5) 3 — warm and honest, but the sting is soft.

## Pick

Candidate 1 (22/25, tied with 3 and 5). Tie-break: it has the strongest
turn and the most concrete image — a laugh in a school corridor becoming a
marriage — and it is the most clearly a line only this older self could
write, naming a place (the library) and a person no one else would know.

## Revision (at least one genuine edit)

Before:
> Kid, the girl who just laughed at your haircut will marry you in eleven years — stop hiding in the library.

After:
> Kid, the girl who just laughed at your haircut will marry you in eleven years — so come out of the library.

Why: "stop hiding in" tells the younger self what not to do; "so come out
of" turns the surprise into an invitation and keeps the sentence moving
forward in the same breath, landing warmer and a beat shorter.
